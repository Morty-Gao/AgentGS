\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
AgentGS separates strategies, shared operations, and tool implementations through a Skill--Capability--Tool hierarchy, enabling local capability composition across Skills. Hypergraph-based completion adds missing intermediate operations using dependencies with provenance and type/tool constraints. Grounding, parameter and artifact binding, and local recovery translate plans into actual invocations. Verified compositions can also form candidate Skills for subsequent strategy reuse.

GeoToolEval results report final-answer agreement and consistency of tool composition and invocation order. AgentGS's main FAS exceeds the evaluated baselines, while configuration comparisons reveal answer and workflow differences. A1 is a partial control, A2's ablation was not activated, A5 changes only part of tool ranking, and some metrics come from different statistical materials. Current differences therefore cannot be interpreted as independent causal contributions of all corresponding modules. Parameter binding, artifact consumption, and scientific-result correctness additionally require execution traces.

The method remains dependent on registered knowledge. Structural and type constraints alone do not fully establish the necessity of inserted operations for the task objective, and local recovery is limited to the existing plan. New Skills preserve compositions without automatically expanding underlying capability or tool coverage; their effects on later performance and planning cost have not been independently evaluated. Future work will combine task semantics and execution feedback to improve completion and plan revision, assess Skill applicability and reuse across tasks, and extend generalization studies to unseen capability combinations and tool changes.
