\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Earth science increasingly combines observational data, computational models, and methods from multiple disciplines~\cite{zheng2025frontiers}. Analyses of climate, resources, and disasters connect data retrieval, alignment, variable derivation, modeling, and verification. Scientific agents built around large language models (LLMs) can help organize these stages into analytical workflows~\cite{feng2026earthagent,ding2025scitoolagent}. Reliable execution requires each operation to receive the appropriate upstream result. Comparing a composite raster's mean with a reference value, for example, requires a statistical operation between raster compositing and scalar comparison. A missing intermediate operation leaves the workflow incomplete even when the selected tools are individually relevant. Orchestration must therefore account for both the operations required by a task and the data exchanged between them.

GeoAgent and Earth-Agent demonstrate multistep geoscientific tool use through planning and execution~\cite{chen2024geoagent,feng2026earthagent}. Planning directly over tools, however, couples workflows to concrete interfaces, while reusing entire strategies limits the extraction of local operations for new tasks. An extracted operation must also retain the prerequisites and implementation constraints imposed by its source strategy. For example, two strategies may share a spatial operation while requiring different preprocessing or input objects. Reusing the operation's name alone does not preserve these requirements. The challenge is to combine \emph{operation-level reuse} with \emph{constraint preservation}, composing operations across strategies without losing the conditions needed for their execution.

AgentGS addresses this challenge through plan composition and completion over skill hypergraphs (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). Its Skill--Capability--Tool representation separates strategies, reusable operations, and concrete implementations. Shared operations connect strategies, while their source-specific dependencies constrain reuse. AgentGS expands selected operations with mandatory predecessors and searches other strategies for compatible branches that connect incomplete fragments. Candidate connections must satisfy prerequisite, data-compatibility, and tool-availability constraints. In the raster example, completion supplies the missing statistical operation and binds its output to the comparison. The system then selects tools, propagates intermediate results, and supports local recovery from execution failures. This separates the organization of an analytical workflow from the choice of its concrete implementations.

Evaluating orchestration also requires examining how answers are obtained. Recent benchmarks assess multistep geoscientific tool use~\cite{krechetova2025geobenchx,feng2026earthagent,yu2026geoagentbench}. We introduce GeoToolEval to pair answer evaluation with reference-workflow comparison across research themes. Tool-call annotations support analysis of selection, arguments, order, and execution efficiency within the same task. Single-step and multistep records distinguish basic tool use from workflows involving intermediate results, while execution traces support investigation of missing operations and failed calls. Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item AgentGS composes and completes workflows across strategies while preserving operation prerequisites and tool constraints before selecting concrete implementations.
\item GeoToolEval provides 603 evaluation records across ten research themes, pairing reference answers with annotated tool workflows to assess outcomes and execution behavior.
\item Experiments show that AgentGS outperforms the evaluated methods in answer quality and tool-workflow agreement.
\end{itemize}
