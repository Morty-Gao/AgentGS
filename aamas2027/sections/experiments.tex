\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
Baseline and ablation configuration comparisons report answer scores and workflow consistency. We distinguish configuration differences from attributable module effects. An illustrative case explains capability composition, parameter and artifact binding, and local recovery; it does not substitute for generalization experiments on unseen tasks or runtime-cost analysis.

\subsection{Tasks and Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}
\paragraph{Data and scope.}
The main experiment covers all 603 GeoToolEval records. Ablations use a fixed 57-record subset. We evaluate answers with FAS and successful tool composition and order with TSF1 and TOS (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}), averaging per-task scores over the relevant records with failures retained. Main results are grouped by reference-chain length; ablations report both the entire subset and its multistep records.

\paragraph{Run configuration and baselines.}
AgentGS uses GPT-5.6 Terra. Main FAS results are calculated from its first complete run over all 603 records, retaining partial credit and failures. Agent baselines GeoAgent~\cite{chen2024geoagent} and EarthAgent~\cite{feng2026earthagent} use the same backbone. Direct-answer language-model baselines are DeepSeek V4 Pro, GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-5.4, Qwen-Plus, and GeoGPT.

TSF1 and TOS compare successful invocations of the three agents in the same tool environment against the same reference chains. Direct-answer language models produce no invocation traces and are compared only by FAS; their process metrics are shown as dashes.

\subsection{Main Results and Baseline Comparison}
\label{sec:main-results}
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} reports FAS, TSF1, and TOS. AgentGS's mean FAS of 0.8731 exceeds GeoAgent's 0.7804, direct answers from the same GPT-5.6 Terra backbone at 0.3137, and the other language-model baselines. Process metrics assess whether answer differences are accompanied by differences in tool composition and invocation order.

\paragraph{Tool selection and invocation order.}
AgentGS obtains mean TSF1 of 0.9170 and TOS of 0.9166 in the present end-to-end experiment over 603 records. All records are retained, including execution failures and incomplete final answers. Successful calls are matched by tool name and multiplicity; the longest common subsequence uses actual execution order. These scores measure aggregate agreement with reference-tool composition and relative order. Table~\ref{tab:main-results} also reports GeoAgent scores of 0.8663/\allowbreak 0.7911 and EarthAgent scores of 0.1182/\allowbreak 0.1390. Parameters, input resources, and scientific results still require other metrics and execution traces. FAS and process metrics provide different outcome and workflow evidence; neither tool names nor order establishes correct artifact consumption.

\paragraph{Chain length and domain differences.}
AgentGS's single-step, two- to three-step, and at-least-four-step FAS scores are 0.9425, 0.7557, and 0.6167. Figure~\ref{fig:performance} reports overall and domain results. Longer chains are concentrated in remote sensing, so domain and chain-length effects overlap and require joint interpretation.

For 119 two- to three-step records, AgentGS obtains TSF1 of 0.9779 and TOS of 0.9832; for 60 records with at least four steps, these are 0.8013 and 0.7785. Both process scores decrease for longer chains, indicating remaining difficulties in required-tool coverage and relative-order preservation.

\subsection{External Evaluation on GeoBenchX}
\label{sec:external-evaluation}
We evaluate AgentGS on GeoBenchX~\cite{krechetova2025geobenchx} after integrating its official tools and constructing dedicated Skills and Capabilities. Tool functions and input/output requirements define capabilities, strategy dependencies, and implementation mappings for planning and orchestration.

The official ReAct baseline and AgentGS are each reported on 202 tasks, both using GPT-5.6 Terra. The same model serves as the single judge under GeoBenchX's reference-solution matching criterion: 0, 1, and 2 indicate mismatch, partial match, and match. Mean per-task score and full-match rate are reported separately from GeoToolEval FAS.

Table~\ref{tab:external-results} reports AgentGS at 1.3762 versus ReAct at 1.1980, a difference of 0.1782 points. Full-match rates are 62.87\% and 53.47\%, a difference of 9.40 percentage points. Both reported values are higher for AgentGS. Although both task counts are supplied, the exact task membership and experimental conditions held constant still require verification before these differences are interpreted as evidence of applicability across tool environments.

\subsection{Ablation Configuration Comparisons}
\label{sec:ablations}
The fixed subset contains 57 records across all ten domains: 22 single-step, 20 two- to three-step, and 15 at-least-four-step records. Multistep records comprise 61.40\%. The experimental design uses the full configuration on the same subset as the reference and disables or changes the mechanisms below. Tables~\ref{tab:ablation} and~\ref{tab:ablation-process} report FAS, TSF1, and TOS for the entire subset and its multistep records. Actual intervention scopes for A1, A2, and A5 are limited, so differences must be interpreted with configuration status and data provenance; they do not uniformly constitute controlled single-module ablations.

\paragraph{Planning and predecessor expansion.}
A1 is designed to retain the same strategies, dependencies, and tools but plan directly at the tool level, providing a structural control for hierarchical decisions. A2 is designed to retain initial selection and dependency declarations while disabling mandatory-predecessor expansion. Because completion can restore omitted predecessors, plan changes and compensatory insertions are also considered. The worksheet, however, identifies A1 as a partial control and states that A2's intended ablation was not activated.

\paragraph{Capability completion.}
A3 retains gap diagnosis but disables supporting-capability insertion, including bridging. A4 disables only directed bridging while retaining other completion mechanisms. Both are examined alongside required-operation coverage, dependency satisfaction, and actual use of upstream artifacts.

\paragraph{Tool selection and recovery.}
A5 is designed to retain requirements, candidate scopes, and execution validation while choosing the first valid tool in a predefined order. Its actual intervention changes only part of the ranking. A6 disables local recovery after execution failure, retaining planning, grounding, and input binding before the first attempt.

\paragraph{FAS differences.}
Relative to the reported Full configuration, A1 has lower overall and multistep FAS by 16.39 and 12.38 percentage points. A2's decreases are 16.27 and 12.38 points, and A3's are 17.63 and 15.31 points. Each multistep decrease is smaller than the corresponding overall decrease. These are configuration-score differences: A1's partial control and A2's inactive ablation preclude directly attributing the differences to hierarchical planning or predecessor expansion. The present comparison does not show larger multistep effects for predecessor expansion or independent completion.

A4 is lower than Full by 2.27 points overall and 4.20 on multistep tasks. It retains other completion mechanisms and changes bridging to examine its incremental role. The comparison with A3 describes different completion configurations; attributing a specific contribution to bridging additionally requires inserted branches, invocation traces, and consistent experimental conditions.

A6 is lower than Full by 4.15 points overall and 7.20 on multistep tasks; linking this difference to recovery requires failure and recovery traces. A5 increases overall FAS from 0.6387 to 0.6415 while reducing multistep FAS from 0.5333 to 0.5184. Since it is a partial ranking intervention, these results describe that local change and do not show a consistent direction of benefit across the two scopes.

\paragraph{Process metrics.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation-process} reports Full TSF1/TOS of 0.9249/\allowbreak 0.9392 on all 57 records and 0.8862/\allowbreak 0.8952 on the 35 multistep records. Full's scores come from the supplementary experiment, while A1--A6 use per-task records in the formal worksheet. All six configurations have lower overall and multistep process scores than Full. This indicates higher agreement between the full configuration and reference-tool composition and relative order; parameter and answer correctness still require FAS and traces.

For multistep tasks, A1's TSF1/TOS changes relative to Full are $-7.15$/\allowbreak $-6.57$ percentage points; A2's are $-5.60$/\allowbreak $-3.64$, and A3's are $-4.98$/\allowbreak $-5.14$. Changes are configuration minus Full. Lower coverage and order scores indicate workflow differences, but the partial A1 control and inactive A2 ablation prevent direct module attribution.

A4 obtains multistep TSF1/TOS of 0.8249/\allowbreak 0.8652, changes of $-6.13$/\allowbreak $-3.00$ points. Compared with A3, its TSF1 is lower but its TOS is higher, so coverage and order do not change in the same direction. The role of bridging requires branch and invocation analysis.

A5 obtains multistep TSF1/TOS of 0.8133/\allowbreak 0.8414, changes of $-7.29$/\allowbreak $-5.38$ points, corresponding to its partial ranking intervention. A6 obtains 0.7652/\allowbreak 0.7985, changes of $-12.10$/\allowbreak $-9.67$ points, with a larger decline in coverage. Assessing recovery requires inspection of failures, retries, and reuse of successful prefixes.
