\section{Ablation and Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}
We examine representation, dependency expansion, completion, tool selection, and recovery. The study reports answer scores alongside tool coverage and order. We distinguish the intended intervention from the intervention actually established by the reported run.

\subsection{Study Design and Intervention Scope}
The fixed subset covers all ten domains and contains 57 records: 22 single-step, 20 two- to three-step, and 15 with at least four reference calls. Its 35 multistep records account for 61.40\%, compared with 29.68\% in the complete dataset. The subset therefore emphasizes orchestration more heavily and should not be treated as a replacement for the 603-record evaluation.

Full is the reference configuration within this study. Its overall and multistep FAS are 0.6387 and 0.5333, respectively. These values belong to the subset experiment; neither its composition nor its run provenance supports directly equating them with the main evaluation.

\paragraph{A1: representation.}
The control asks the model to plan directly in tool instances while retaining strategies, dependencies, and candidate tools. It targets the organization of planning decisions rather than removing all strategy knowledge. The reported run implements only a partial control. Its score difference therefore cannot isolate the value of hierarchical representation.

\paragraph{A2: mandatory-predecessor expansion.}
The intended intervention retains selected requirements and dependency declarations but disables automatic predecessor closure. Completion remains available and may restore omitted prerequisites. The supplied result record states that the intended A2 intervention was not activated. Its numbers are retained for provenance but are not evidence for an expansion effect.

\paragraph{A3 and A4: completion.}
A3 keeps gap diagnosis but disables supporting-capability insertion, including bridging. A4 disables only directed bridging while retaining the other completion mechanisms. Their scopes distinguish general support insertion from the additional connection supplied by an inactive provider Skill.

\paragraph{A5: implementation selection.}
The intended control chooses the first eligible tool in a fixed order while preserving operation requirements, candidate scope, and argument validation. The reported run changes only part of ranking, so it does not establish the effect of removing all selection optimization.

\paragraph{A6: execution recovery.}
A6 disables recovery after a scientific execution failure. Initial planning, grounding, and binding remain enabled. The relevant contrast is whether failure feedback can repair an execution or trigger replanning, rather than whether initial inputs are validated.

\subsection{Planning Representation and Dependency Expansion}
Relative to Full, A1 lowers overall FAS by 16.39 percentage points and multistep FAS by 12.38 points. Its multistep TSF1/TOS are 0.8147/\allowbreak 0.8295, compared with Full's 0.8862/\allowbreak 0.8952. Thus, the reported configuration differs in both answers and successful-call sequences. Because A1 is only a partial control, these changes cannot be assigned solely to planning at the capability level.

A2 has overall and multistep FAS decreases of 16.27 and 12.38 points. Its multistep TSF1/TOS are 0.8302/\allowbreak 0.8588. The inactive intervention prevents interpreting these differences as the effect of predecessor expansion, regardless of their magnitude.

There is also a structural interaction between expansion and completion. If completion inserts a prerequisite omitted by the expansion control, the final plan may remain unchanged even when the control is active. Conversely, a model can explicitly include necessary operations without automatic closure. Isolating expansion requires inspecting initial requirements, predecessor additions, support additions, and unresolved gaps, rather than inferring the intervention from the final score alone.

\subsection{Supporting-Capability Completion and Bridging}
A3 obtains overall FAS of 0.4624 and multistep FAS of 0.3802, decreases of 17.63 and 15.31 points from Full. Both scopes show lower answer agreement. The multistep decrease is smaller than the overall decrease, so this result does not support a claim that completion has a larger effect on multistep tasks in this subset.

A4 obtains overall and multistep FAS of 0.6160 and 0.4913, decreases of 2.27 and 4.20 points. It retains more of the Full configuration's answer performance than A3. The two configurations remove different sets of mechanisms, however, so their difference is not an independently measured additive contribution of bridging.

Process scores further separate their behavior. On multistep records, A3 obtains TSF1/TOS of 0.8364/\allowbreak 0.8438, whereas A4 obtains 0.8249/\allowbreak 0.8652. A4 has lower coverage agreement but higher order agreement than A3. This divergence shows why a single process score would obscure changes in successful-call sequences.

A bridge-specific explanation requires evidence that the task exposes a disconnected pair of active fragments, an admissible provider branch was available, and the inserted operation consumed and produced the expected artifacts. The case in Appendix~\ref{sec:case-study} explains these conditions for a composite-raster workflow. It is a mechanism illustration rather than a measured frequency of bridge use across the dataset.

\subsection{Tool Selection and Execution Recovery}
A5 increases overall FAS from 0.6387 to 0.6415 but decreases multistep FAS from 0.5333 to 0.5184. Its multistep TSF1/TOS are 0.8133/\allowbreak 0.8414, decreases of 7.29/\allowbreak 5.38 percentage points. Answer and process changes therefore do not have a uniform direction across the reported scopes. In view of the partial ranking intervention, these results describe the available configuration rather than establish a general selection benefit.

A6 obtains overall and multistep FAS of 0.5972 and 0.4613, decreases of 4.15 and 7.20 points. Its multistep TSF1/TOS are 0.7652/\allowbreak 0.7985, decreases of 12.10/\allowbreak 9.67 points. The coverage decline is the largest among the six reported multistep configurations. This is consistent with recovery affecting how much of a successful-call sequence is completed, but the aggregate does not identify which failed calls were recoverable.

Trace-level attribution should distinguish argument corrections, alternative implementations, replanned operations, and exhaustion of execution budgets. A resumed scalar computation and a rerun of a file-producing step also have different costs. The reported scores contain no matched latency or token-cost analysis, so they do not establish the efficiency of recovery.

\subsection{Joint Interpretation of Answer and Workflow Evidence}
Full has the highest reported overall and multistep process scores: 0.9249/\allowbreak 0.9392 and 0.8862/\allowbreak 0.8952, respectively. Its process scores come from the supplementary 57-record experiment, while A1--A6 use records from the formal worksheet. The intervention and provenance limitations remain relevant even when all process differences favor Full.

The combined results identify three questions for mechanistic analysis. Which declared operations are missing before execution? Which consumers receive the intended upstream artifacts? Which failures can be repaired without changing the scientific objective? These questions connect the model's decisions to scientific outputs more directly than tool-name agreement alone. The current aggregates establish configuration differences; a causal account of individual components additionally requires matched runs and aligned execution traces.
